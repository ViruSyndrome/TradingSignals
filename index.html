How to Trade Moonshots

Moonshots are highly volatile momentum plays on 4H charts. Trade them differently than daily core signals.

  • Timing chips: Prefer 4H CONFIRM (EMA / breakout retest). WAIT means don’t chase an extended rip — Strong Buy may be downgraded until the retest.
  • Watch vs scanner: Auto-scan fills the Moonshots tab only. It does not star coins for you. Star ⭐ only ideas you want to revisit; Lock 🔒 after you buy.
  • Review window: Faded setups are cleaned after ~4 hours unless you Watched or Locked them.
  • Tight Stops: ~5% max stop. If the signal flips to SELL, close immediately.

5m Scalps

Short EMA-pullback plays on liquid volatile alts. They appear only on the ⚡ 5m Scalps tab (not duplicated on All).

  • Do not buy the instant a coin appears. Wait for 5m CONFIRM (EMA retest + reclaim). WAIT = still extended / forming — watch only.
  • Prefer S.BUY + CONFIRM. Plain BUY without CONFIRM is weaker — paper it first.
  • Size & exits: Tiny size. Place stop immediately (~1.5% cap), bank ~2R, time-stop ~30–60 minutes. If it doesn’t move, flat.
  • Empty tab is normal in chop — the scanner is supposed to stay quiet.

SMC, Wyckoff & Volume Profile (POC)

These are heuristic score boosters — useful pattern detectors, not full institutional “smart money” systems. They can inflate a BUY score but still cannot mint a trusted Strong Buy alone (S.BUY still needs score ≥ 4, 100% confidence, cool RSI).

  • 💎 SMC Liquidity Sweep (~+2 / +3): Price briefly takes liquidity below a local low, then rejects hard (optional volume confirm). Idea: stop-hunt then reclaim.
  • 🐋 Wyckoff Spring (~+1.5): Tight range → fake breakdown below the floor → reclaim on stronger volume. Idea: shakeout before markup.
  • 🛡️ VPOC / Point of Control (~+1 / +1.5 on scalps): Price sits just above the highest-volume price bin in the recent window — treated as a volume “wall” / support bounce.
  • Validation: Weekly Action runs node backtest.js --smc-ablation (ON vs OFF). Until that consistently helps, treat these factors as experimental.
💡 Honest wording: “Inspired by” SMC / Wyckoff / volume profile — not a claim that whales are detected in real time.

The 7-Day Hold Limit

Patience pays — but capital should not sit idle. Live TrendRunner exits use a 50/50 plan: bank half at a fixed take-profit, trail the rest so winners can run, with a maximum hold window on leftovers.

  • The Delay: Daily signals often need a few sessions to develop. Do not panic-sell on day 2 because of normal noise.
  • Scale A (50%): Sell OCO — bank at +10% with the suggested ATR stop on that half.
  • Scale B (50%): Trailing / ATR trail on the runner; move stop to breakeven after Scale A fills — this is how larger runners stay possible.
  • The 7-Day Cap: If leftovers have not exited within 7 days, close them and free capital.
💡 Strategy: Place both legs at entry, lock the coin in Holdings, and walk away.

Automating Exits with OCO Orders

OCO stands for One-Cancels-the-Other. It is the single most important tool on Binance to protect your capital and lock in profits while you sleep.

  • What it is: An OCO order places a Take-Profit limit order and a Stop-Loss order at the exact same time. If one hits, Binance automatically cancels the other.
  • How to use it on Binance: Go to the Trade screen, change the order type from 'Limit' to 'OCO'.
  • Price (Take Profit): Enter the target price where you want to secure your gains.
  • Stop Limit: Enter your emergency exit price. If the market crashes, this automatically sells your coins to prevent a massive loss.
💡 Pro Tip: Moonshots require much tighter Stop-Losses because they are highly volatile. Never buy a Moonshot without an OCO.

RSI — Relative Strength Index

The RSI measures how fast and how much a price has moved recently. It runs on a scale of 0 to 100.

  • Below 30 — The asset is oversold. Sellers have pushed it too far. A bounce often follows. This is a buying signal.
  • 30 to 70 — Normal range. No extreme signal either way. Watch for direction.
  • Above 70 — The asset is overbought. Buyers are overextended. A pullback often follows. This is a selling signal.
💡 Beginner tip: RSI is most reliable in sideways/ranging markets. In strong trends, RSI can stay overbought or oversold for a long time!
📈

EMA vs SMA (The Day Trader's Secret)

Moving averages smooth out price noise. We use a powerful Dual Strategy:

  • SMA (Macro Trend): Simple Moving Average (50 & 200). It is slow and smooth. We use it to see if the overall market is in a long-term bull or bear trend. If price is above the 50 SMA, it's safe to buy.
  • EMA (Day Trading): Exponential Moving Average (9 & 21). It places more weight on recent prices, reacting instantly to market shifts.
  • Bullish Trigger: When the fast 9 EMA crosses above the 21 EMA. This is your exact signal to enter a day trade (if the Macro Trend is also bullish).
  • Bearish Trigger: When the 9 EMA crosses below the 21 EMA. This is your signal to exit or short.
💡 Strategy: Use the SMA for the Direction (Should I buy?), and the EMA for the Timing (When exactly should I buy?).
〽️

MACD — Moving Average Convergence Divergence

MACD shows the direction and strength of a trend. It has two lines: the MACD line and a Signal line, plus a histogram.

  • Bullish Crossover — MACD line crosses ABOVE the signal line. This is a buy trigger used by millions of traders.
  • Bearish Crossover — MACD line crosses BELOW the signal line. This is a sell trigger.
  • Histogram — Shows the gap between the two lines. Green bars = growing bullish momentum. Red bars = growing bearish momentum.
💡 Beginner tip: MACD crossovers work best as a confirmation signal — use them together with RSI for higher accuracy.
🎯

Bollinger Bands

Bollinger Bands show volatility and relative price levels. They are 2 standard deviations above and below a 20-day moving average.

  • Price near Lower Band — The price is statistically low relative to recent history. Potential bounce (buy zone).
  • Price near Upper Band — The price is statistically high. Potential reversal (sell zone).
  • Band Squeeze — Bands narrow → a big move is coming. Direction unknown until breakout.
  • %B indicator — 0% = at lower band, 100% = at upper band, 50% = at middle.

The Most Important Rule: Risk Management

No strategy works 100% of the time. What separates successful traders from losing ones is how they manage losses.

  • 1-2% Rule — Never risk more than 1–2% of your total trading capital on a single trade.
  • Always set a Stop-Loss — Before entering any trade, decide your maximum loss. Stick to it.
  • Risk-to-Reward — Aim for a minimum 1:2 ratio. Risk ₹1000 to potentially gain ₹2000.
  • Start Small — Begin with small amounts while learning. There's no prize for losing big fast.
  • Never Average Down on Losses — If a trade goes against you, don't buy more just to lower your average.
💡 The #1 beginner mistake: Letting small losses become big losses because you didn't set a stop-loss.

Live Exit Policy: 10% TP & 7-Day Hold

Dashboard cards, OCO helpers, Telegram/X alerts, and the visual backtester all read the same exit policy from config.

  • Bank +10% on half: Keeps a reliable cash-flow leg so you are not forced to hope every trade moons.
  • Trail the other half: Lets occasional 40%+ runners happen without raising the fixed TP on your full size.
  • The 7-Day Time Limit: If a trade does not hit +10% within 7 days, treat capital as better deployed elsewhere.
  • Dynamic Stop-Losses (ATR): Stops use ~2× ATR so volatile coins get more room than quiet ones.
💡 Strategy: At entry place Scale A OCO (half @ +10%) and Scale B trail (half). Max hold leftovers = 7 days.

Psychology & Pro Tips for Success

The hardest part of trading isn't the math; it's managing your own emotions. Here is how the pros survive and thrive:

  • Trade the Chart, Not the News: News is often priced in before you even read it. Technical indicators show what people are actually doing with their money.
  • Avoid FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out): If a stock just shot up 50% in a day, you missed the entry. Wait for a pullback or find the next setup. Chasing green candles usually leads to buying the top.
  • Keep a Trading Journal: Use Holdings (exact Binance fills + lots) and I followed this in Signal Log for hit/miss tracking. A Binance trade-history dump still helps for full fee-accurate review.
  • Accept that Losses are Just Business Expenses: Even the best algorithms only win 55-60% of the time. The goal isn't to win every trade, but to make sure your winners are bigger than your losers (Risk-to-Reward).
  • Trust the Process, But Verify: Backtest in your head. Look at historical charts when the MACD crossed over or RSI was below 30. See what happened next to build confidence in the indicators.
💡 Pro Tip: Boredom is your friend. Good trading should feel systematic and almost boring. If your heart is racing, your position size is too big.
